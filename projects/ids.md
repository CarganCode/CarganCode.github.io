## BSc Dissertation (2018 - 2019)

My undergraduate dissertation focused on forecasting solar irradiance across UK solar farms using satellite weather imagery and ground meteorological observations. I developed ConvLSTM-based models in TensorFlow and Apache Spark data pipelines, and compared global and local architectures against a gradient-boosted tree industry baseline. This work received the Best Dissertation 2019 award for the highest mark in the year.

#### Abstract

> Adoption of renewable energy sources is a key step in curbing the effects of climate change.
> Moving away from traditional energy generation processes presents many challenges.
> Unlike coal and natural gas, many renewable energy sources and specifically solar operate intermittently and have abrupt changes in output.
> This variability presents a problem both for generators, trying to meet contractual obligations, and for grid operators endeavouring to ensure a consistent load.
> Having a system capable of producing accurate predictions of solar irradiance would be of use to both parties.
> This project aims to build such a system, leveraging advancements in both machine learning and big data processing.
